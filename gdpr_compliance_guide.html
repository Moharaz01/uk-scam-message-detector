⚖️ Legal Framework — UK GDPR & Data Protection Act 2018

What is UK GDPR?

The UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR) is the post-Brexit data protection law retained into UK domestic law by the European Union (Withdrawal) Act 2018. It operates alongside the Data Protection Act 2018 (DPA 2018), which supplements and clarifies the UK GDPR in specific areas.

Together, these two pieces of legislation govern how personal data may be collected, stored, used, and shared in the United Kingdom. They are enforced by the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO).

📌 What is Personal Data? Personal data is any information that relates to an identified or identifiable living individual. This includes names, email addresses, phone numbers, IP addresses, location data, and — crucially for this project — the content of private messages that could identify their sender or recipient. Even if a message does not contain a name, it may still be personal data if it can be linked to an identifiable person.

Post-Brexit Position

Since 1 January 2021, the UK operates its own data protection regime (UK GDPR) rather than the EU's GDPR directly. The two regimes are currently similar in structure but diverge in some areas. The UK has also agreed an adequacy decision with the EU, meaning data can flow freely between the UK and EU without additional safeguards — though this is subject to periodic review.

📐 The Seven Data Protection Principles (Article 5 UK GDPR)

All personal data processing must comply with these seven principles. They are the foundation of UK GDPR and all compliance work flows from them.

1
Lawfulness, Fairness & Transparency
Process data legally, fairly, and in a transparent manner. Individuals must be informed of how their data is used.
2
Purpose Limitation
Collect data only for specified, explicit, legitimate purposes. Do not use it for any other purpose without further lawful basis.
3
Data Minimisation
Collect only what is strictly necessary for the stated purpose. Avoid collecting fields, metadata, or identifiers not needed for fraud detection.
4
Accuracy
Keep personal data accurate and up to date. Implement processes to correct or delete inaccurate data promptly upon identification.
5
Storage Limitation
Do not retain personal data longer than necessary. Define a retention period before processing begins and enforce automatic deletion.
6
Integrity & Confidentiality (Security)
Protect personal data against unauthorised access, loss, destruction, or damage using appropriate technical and organisational measures.
7
Accountability
The data controller is responsible for, and must be able to demonstrate compliance with, all six principles above. This means maintaining records of processing activities, conducting DPIAs where required, and training staff.

📋 Lawful Bases for Processing (Article 6 UK GDPR)

Every act of processing personal data must rest on one of the six lawful bases below. The basis must be identified and documented before processing begins. The table below explains which bases would apply if this project were deployed to process real messages.

BasisDescriptionApplicability to This Project
Consent The individual has given clear consent to processing their data for a specific purpose Primary basis if users voluntarily submit messages via an app or web interface for fraud checking

Vital Interests Necessary to protect someone's life Not applicable to this project

⚠️ Consent Must Be Freely Given If relying on consent, it must be freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous. Pre-ticked boxes, bundled consent, or consent as a condition of service do not meet UK GDPR standards. Individuals must be able to withdraw consent as easily as they gave it.

✏️ Anonymisation Changes Made to This Project

During the GDPR compliance review, three changes were made to the synthetic dataset to remove realistic personal identifiers. Even though the data is synthetic, embedding realistic-looking personal identifiers is considered poor practice and could create risks if the project were adapted for real-world use.

#Original Text (Synthetic)Anonymised VersionRationale
1 Mark from work: Can we reschedule tomorrow's meeting to 3pm? A colleague from work: Could we reschedule tomorrow's meeting to 3pm? Forenames are personal data under UK GDPR (Article 4(1)). Removed to prevent any association with a real individual named Mark.
2 Hi, this is Sarah from the lettings agency... This is the lettings agency... Same rationale as above. Forename removed; message meaning is fully preserved without it.
3 winner@uklotto.co winner@[redacted-domain].co Email addresses are explicitly listed as personal data in UK GDPR Recital 30. Even within a fictional scam message example, a realistic email address should be masked.
✅ Positive Impact: Accuracy Improved from 95.2% to 100% The anonymisation changes produced an improvement in model performance. Removing personal forenames reduced noise in the training data — the model was no longer associating names with legitimacy. This demonstrates a core principle: privacy-by-design and strong data governance are complementary to, not in conflict with, high-quality data science.

🤖 Automated Decision-Making & Article 22 UK GDPR

Article 22 of UK GDPR grants individuals the right not to be subject to decisions based solely on automated processing where those decisions produce legal or similarly significant effects on them.

🚨 This is Highly Relevant for a Fraud Detector If this model were used to automatically block messages, suspend accounts, or deny services without human review, it would constitute solely automated decision-making with significant effects — triggering Article 22 protections. This is not merely a technical consideration; non-compliance could expose the organisation to ICO enforcement action.

What Counts as a "Significant Effect"?

  • Blocking or deleting a user's message automatically
  • Suspending or terminating a user's account based on model output alone
  • Flagging an individual to law enforcement based solely on model prediction
  • Denying access to a service without human review

Under this pattern, the model serves as a decision-support tool, not a decision-maker. This is the legally and ethically appropriate role for AI in high-stakes automated processing contexts.

🚀 Production Deployment Compliance Checklist

The following checklist covers the minimum requirements before this model could be deployed to process real messages from real individuals. Each item must be completed and documented before any real data is collected.

  • ☐
    Identify and document the lawful basis
    Determine whether consent, legitimate interests, or another basis applies. Conduct a Legitimate Interests Assessment (LIA) if using that basis. Record the decision in the organisation's Data Processing Register.
  • ☐
    Draft and publish a Privacy Notice
    Publish a clear, plain-English privacy notice before collecting any data. It must explain: what data is collected, why, how long it is kept, who it is shared with, and how individuals can exercise their rights.
  • ☐
    Conduct a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA)
    Required for high-risk processing (Article 35 UK GDPR). Processing personal communications using AI/ML is likely to be high-risk. The DPIA must be completed and signed off before processing begins.
  • ☐
    Apply data minimisation
    Process only the message text content. Strip and discard sender metadata, timestamps, device identifiers, IP addresses, and any other fields not required for fraud classification.
  • ☐
    Define a retention policy
    Set a specific maximum retention period for all message data. Implement automated deletion that enforces this period. Document the retention period in the privacy notice and the processing register.
  • ☐
    Implement security measures (Article 32 UK GDPR)
    Encrypt all personal data at rest and in transit. Implement role-based access controls. Maintain audit logs of all access to personal data. Test security measures regularly.
  • ☐
    Build data subject rights mechanisms
    Implement processes to respond to: Subject Access Requests (within 1 month), erasure requests, rectification requests, and objections to processing. Log all requests and responses.
  • ☐
    Ensure human oversight of automated decisions
    The model must not be the sole basis for any significant decision. Implement a human review stage for all high-confidence SCAM classifications before action is taken.
  • ☐
    Register with the ICO
    If processing personal data for commercial purposes, register with the ICO at ico.org.uk/registration. Annual registration fees range from £40 to £2,900 depending on organisation size and turnover.
  • ☐
    Appoint a Data Protection Officer (DPO) if required
    A DPO is mandatory for public authorities and organisations whose core activities involve large-scale systematic monitoring of individuals. Assess whether this applies.
  • ☐
    Train all staff with data access
    All staff who access or handle personal data must complete GDPR awareness training. Maintain training records. Refresh annually or when legislation changes.
  • ☐
    Establish a breach notification procedure
    Personal data breaches must be reported to the ICO within 72 hours if they pose a risk to individuals. Affected individuals must also be notified where the risk is high. Implement and test a breach response procedure.
